### Build Out Logic Regarding Types Of Capital

- **Replace**  `A.3.3 First Loss Capital (Portal)` to read as follows:
    - **Risk Capital** *(Article)* - Star Agents who invest capital from Sky’s Collateral Portfolio using the Allocation System Primitive must hold Risk Capital to protect Sky from potential losses on these investments. This Article sets forth the framework governing Risk Capital.
        - **Conceptual Framework** *(Section)* - This Section defines the conceptual framework for Risk Capital.
            - **Required Risk Capital** *(Core)* - The documents herein define the conceptual framework for determining the amount of Risk Capital that Star Agents are required to hold to protect Sky from risks on their investments.
                - **Risk Factors** *(Core)* - The documents herein define how Risk Capital required for different categories of risk are calculated and combined to determine total Risk Capital required.
                    - **Introduction** *(Core)* - Different risk factors may apply to varying degrees to particular types of investments. For example, investments through decentralized lending markets are subject to credit risk but not legal risk. As such, the risk framework contains methodologies for calculating the Risk Capital required with respect to different risk factors. The Risk Capital requirements for each risk factor relevant to an investment are then added together to arrive at the total Risk Capital required.
                    - **Process** *(Core)* - The documents herein provide an overview of the process for estimating Risk Capital for different types of investments.
                        - **Determining Relevant Risk Factors** *(Core)* - For each type of investment, the relevant risk factors should be determined. This determination should be based on a fundamental analysis of the extent to which each of these risk factors is relevant to the investment.
                        - **Estimating Risk Factors** *(Core)* - For each risk factor that is relevant to an investment, the required Risk Capital to protect Sky from potential losses on that investment should be estimated.
                        - **Calculating Aggregate Required Risk Capital** *(Core)* - Once the Risk Capital requirements associated with each risk factor are calculated, they should be added to arrive at a total Risk Capital requirement.
                    - **Application To Lending Markets** *(Core)* - The near term focus of the framework is on lending markets such as SparkLend. For lending markets, the relevant risk factors are financial risk, smart contract risk, and admin risk.
                    - **Incorporation Of Additional Risk Factors** *(Core)* - Additional risk factors, including legal risk and oracle risk, will be incorporated in future iterations of the risk framework.
                - **Financial Risk** *(Core)* - The documents herein define the conceptual framework for determining the Risk Capital required to protect Sky from financial risk arising from investments by Star Agents of funds from Sky’s Collateral Portfolio. The financial risk framework is currently focused on credit risk. In future iterations of the risk framework, financial risk will be disaggregated into credit risk, liquidity risk, and market risk.
                    - **Credit Risk** *(Core)* - Credit risk is the risk that a loan or other debt instrument will not be repaid according to agreed terms.
                    - **Exposure And Rehypothecation** *(Core)* - In lending markets, an asset originally posted as collateral may be subsequently lent as collateral for additional loans, increasing overall exposure. Methodologies for estimating risk should take into account this overall exposure. To the extent that other investments have similar mechanisms that can increase overall risk, these should be incorporated into the calculation of required Risk Capital.
                        - **Depth Levels** *(Core)* - To capture exposures related to rehypothecation, the Risk Capital framework utilizes the concept of “depth levels”. The first depth level relates to exposures that have not been rehypothecated. The second depth level relates to additional exposures from the rehypothecated portion of first depth level exposures. The possibility of further lending from re-lent positions should be accounted for, either as an adjustment to the second depth level exposure or through incorporation of additional depth levels.
                        - **Tracking Effective Exposures** *(Core)* - Effective exposures should reflect both non-rehypothecated exposures from the first depth level as well as rehypothecated exposures from the second depth level.
                    - **Core Metrics** *(Core)* - The documents herein define core metrics that will be used in the Financial Risk framework.
                        - **Probability Of Default** *(Core)* - The Probability Of Default is the probability that a loan will default. This is an important variable in determining Risk Capital required for Credit Risk.
                            - **Current Limitations** *(Core)* - The Probability Of Default is currently estimated assuming that returns of the underlying asset are normally distributed. Future iterations of the risks framework should explore other probability distributions to better incorporate “fat tails” in asset returns.
                        - **Loss Given Default** *(Core)* - The Loss Given Default is the estimate of the loss that will occur on a loan conditional on it having defaulted. It is typically estimated at some confidence level, for example that in 99.9% of cases, the actual loss will not exceed the specified value. This is an important variable in determining Risk Capital Required for Credit Risk.
                            - **Current Limitations** *(Core)* - The Loss Given Default is currently estimated assuming that returns of the underlying asset are normally distributed. Future iterations of the risk framework should explore other probability distributions to better incorporate “fat tails” in asset returns.
                        - **Asset Correlation** *(Core)* - Asset Correlation is a factor that accounts for the fact that defaults on loans are not completely correlated, so some loans in a portfolio may default while others continue to perform.
                            - **Current Limitations** *(Core)* - The current implementation of Asset Correlation uses parameters from the Basel Committee on Banking Supervision that were estimated for commercial loans (https://www.bis.org/bcbs/irbriskweight.pdf). These should be replaced by parameters that are based on historical correlation of crypto loans in a future iteration of the risk framework.
                        - **Risk Capital** *(Core)* - The Risk Capital required for Credit Risk is a function of the Probability of Default, the Loss Given Default, and the Asset Correlation. The methodology for calculating Risk Capital required for Credit Risk based on these variables is specified in the implementation of the Risk Capital framework.
                - **Smart Contract Risk** *(Core)* - The documents herein define the conceptual framework for determining the Risk Capital required to protect Sky from smart contract risk arising from investments by Star Agents of funds from Sky’s Collateral Portfolio.
                    - **Definition** *(Core)* - Smart Contract Risk refers to the risk that an investment through the Allocation System will experience a loss of funds due to a bug or technical exploit. This risk may arise from the protocol invested in or other aspects of the investment such as bridging requirements.
                    - **Development** *(Core)* - The conceptual framework for smart contract risk is still being developed, so Star Agents are not currently required to hold any Risk Capital for Smart Contract Risk; they are only required to hold Risk Capital for Credit Risk.
                    - **Short Term Review By Operational GovOps** *(Core)* - Until the framework for Smart Contract Risk has been implemented, Operational Executor Agents can decline to operationalize any Allocation System Primitive deployment that they deem poses excessive smart contract risk, unless the Agent maintains 100% Risk Capital for that deployment.
                - **Admin Risk** *(Core)* - The documents herein define the conceptual framework for determining the Risk Capital required to protect Sky from admin risk arising from investments by Star Agents of funds from Sky’s Collateral Portfolio.
                    - **Definition** *(Core)* - Admin Risk refers to the risk that an investment through the Allocation System will experience a loss of funds due to abuse of privileged access to a protocol. This risk may arise from a multisig or another actor that has the ability to modify the protocol or access funds without going through a governance process.
                    - **Development** *(Core)* - The conceptual framework for admin risk is still being developed, so Star Agents are not currently required to hold any Risk Capital for Admin Risk; they are only required to hold Risk Capital for Credit Risk.
                    - **Short Term Review By Operational GovOps** *(Core)* - Until the framework for Admin Risk has been implemented, Operational Executor Agents can decline to operationalize any Allocation System Primitive deployment that they deem poses excessive admin risk, unless the Agent maintains 100% Risk Capital for that deployment.
                - **Near-Term Framework Scope** *(Core)* - In the near term, the Risk Capital framework applies only to capital deployments into lending markets such as SparkLend. The framework will be expanded to cover additional asset classes over time.
                    - **Other Exposures** *(Core)* - The documents herein specify the near term logic regarding Risk Capital requirements for types of assets other than lending markets.
                    - **Restricted Exposures** *(Core)* - The documents herein define types of exposures that are either restricted or completely prohibited until the conceptual framework and its implementation are expanded to include them.
            - **Eligible Assets** *(Core*) - The documents herein define the conceptual framework for eligible assets that Star Agents may hold that count toward their required Risk Capital. Agents failing to maintain the required level of capital will be subject to penalties.
                - **Types Of Risk Capital** *(Core)* - Risk Capital comprises two main categories based on loss absorption seniority: First Loss Capital (FLC) and Senior Risk Capital (SRC), further divided by source.
                    - **First Loss Capital** *(Core)* - First Loss Capital (FLC) is the first capital to absorb losses on investments under the Allocation System. First Loss Capital must experience 100% losses before any losses are absorbed by Senior Risk Capital.
                        - **First Loss Capital Types** (*Core*) - The documents herein define the types of First Loss Capital.
                            - **Internal First Loss Capital (IFLC)** *(Core)* - Internal First Loss Capital is capital owned by the Star Agent itself. It serves as the foundation for the Star's risk capacity: the amount of IFLC directly dictates the maximum External FLC the Star can source (governed by the External Per Internal ratio) and provides the primary capacity for enabling Senior Risk Capital (via the Senior Per First Loss ratio), while also bearing the initial impact of any losses (the Tip FLC mechanism). IFLC must be eligible assets held in the Star Agent’s designated treasury account (e.g., SubProxy), controlled via its Operational Executor Agent.
                                
                                See A.3.3 - External Per Internal Ratio, A.3.3 - Senior Per First Loss Ratio, and A.3.3 - FLC Loss Allocation Rules. 
                                
                                - **Types Of Eligible Assets for IFLC** *(Core)* - The documents herein define the types of assets that qualify as Internal First Loss Capital.
                                    - These types will be specified in a future iteration of the Risk Framework.
                            - **Star-External First Loss Capital (SEFLC)** *(Core)* - SEFLC is FLC rented from another Star Agent via the [A.2.4 - First Loss Capital Rental Primitive](https://sky-atlas.powerhouse.io/A.2.3.9.2_First_Loss_Capital_Rental_Primitive/1b2f2ff0-8d73-80ee-ba94-e1f7b69e1f36|9e1ff936a6cb). The capital remains in the lender's treasury but counts towards the borrower's FLC amount. SEFLC is represented by a registered Ecosystem Accord signifying that a specified amount of the lending Agent's FLC (held within the lender's treasury) is designated to count towards the borrowing Agent's FLC requirement for the duration and terms specified in the Accord.
                            - **Tokenized External First Loss Capital (TEFLC)** *(Core)* - Tokenized External First Loss Capital is capital provided by external capital providers depositing sUSDS into the Tokenized External First Loss Capital smart contract, from which Stars can encumber funds. The details of this contract will be specified in a future iteration of the Atlas.
                                - **Definition Of TEFLC System** (*Core*) - The documents herein describe at a high-level the working of the TEFLC system.
                                    - **Deployment Of Smart Contract** *(Core)* - To access Tokenized External First Loss Capital, the Star Agent first deploys a Tokenized External First Loss Capital smart contract. This will be available as a Sky Primitive in a future iteration of the Atlas.
                                    - **Users Deposit sUSDS Into Smart Contract** *(Core)* - The smart contract allows users to deposit sUSDS, taking on the risk of losses on the Star Agent’s investments in return for the potential of higher yield than the Sky Savings Rate.
                                    - **Star Agent Can Encumber Funds From Smart Contract To Count Towards Capital Requirements** *(Core)* - Star Agents, through their Operational Executor Agents, have the ability to encumber a portion of the funds in the smart contract, essentially “borrowing” the funds to serve as First Loss Capital. The encumbered portion of funds in the smart contract qualifies as Tokenized External First Loss Capital towards the Star Agent’s required Risk Capital.
                                    - **Interest Rate On Smart Contract Determined By Percentage Of Encumbered Funds** *(Core)* - The interest rate paid to users depositing funds into the smart contract is a function of the percentage of funds in the smart contract that are encumbered. This is similar to how the interest rate in decentralized lending markets is a function of the utilization rate. In this way, a Star Agent encumbering a high percentage of the available funds will pay a higher interest rate, naturally incentivizing additional funds to be deposited.
                                    - **Users Can Withdraw Unencumbered Funds From Smart Contract** *(Core)* - Users can withdraw funds from the smart contract so long as there are unencumbered funds available. Withdrawals increase the percentage of funds that are encumbered, increasing the interest rate and naturally incentivizing other users to deposit funds.
                        - **FLC Loss Allocation Rules** *(Core)* - This document defines the conceptual framework for distributing losses within the FLC layer.
                            - **Initial Loss Absorption By “Tip FLC”** *(Core)* - The Tip FLC mechanism dictates that losses from a distinct risk event are first absorbed entirely by the Star Agent's Internal First Loss Capital (IFLC), up to a specific threshold amount. This threshold amount is calculated as a percentage of Total FLC and is specified in A.3.3 - FLC Loss Allocation Parameters.
                                
                                Only losses exceeding this threshold amount trigger absorption by other First Loss Capital components - specifically, the remaining IFLC and External First Loss Capital. This initial absorption solely by IFLC ensures that Star Agents are highly incentivized to prevent losses and avoids the need to resolve disputes regarding the allocation of small losses to EFLC providers. 
                                
                            - **Post-Tip FLC Loss Allocation** *(Core)* - If a loss event exceeds the Tip FLC amount, the remaining loss is allocated pro-rata across the rest of the FLC capital. This includes the remaining Internal First Loss Capital (Total IFLC minus the Tip amount) and all External First Loss Capital (Star External First Loss Capital plus Tokenized External First Loss Capital).
                            - **Per-Event Basis** *(Core)* - The FLC loss allocation rules are applied independently based on the total losses attributed to each distinct risk event. The concept of “per-event basis” is crucial; the classification of a distinct risk event (classifying losses as one large event versus multiple smaller ones) can dictate whether the Tip FLC threshold is breached.
                                
                                Breaching the threshold activates the pro-rata sharing mechanism for any losses exceeding the Tip FLC amount, distributing such excess losses between both the remaining Internal FLC (IFLC) and all External FLC (EFLC). Conversely, if the threshold is not breached for an event, the loss is contained entirely within the Tip layer and absorbed solely by IFLC.  
                                
                                The operational process for the determination of distinct risk events will be defined in a future iteration of the Atlas. 
                                
                    - **Senior Risk Capital** *(Core)* - Senior Risk Capital (SRC) is protected from losses relative to First Loss Capital. Senior Risk Capital only begins to absorb losses after First Loss Capital has experienced 100% losses and absorbs 100% of losses thereafter.
                        
                        Senior Risk Capital can be originated from Sky Core on a monthly basis as part of the Monthly Settlement Cycle; Star Agents can also rent Senior Risk Capital from each other.
                        
                        - **Senior Risk Capital Types** (*Core*) -  The documents herein define the types of Senior Risk Capital.
                            - **Internal Senior Risk Capital** *(Core)* - Internal Senior Risk Capital (ISRC) is capital provided from Sky Core’s Surplus Buffer. The composition of Internal Senior Risk Capital will be specified in more detail in a future iteration of the Atlas.
                            - **External Senior Risk Capital** *(Core)* - External Senior Risk Capital (ESRC) is capital provided from the srUSDS smart contract, which allow users to provide USDS to Sky Core to serve as senior risk capital in exchange for higher returns. The mechanics of this are specified in A.3.3 - External Senior Risk Capital And srUSDS System.
                - **Risk-Capital Sourcing Ratios** *(Core)* - The documents herein define the required relationship and constraints between the different types of Risk Capital. These ensure that the Star Agent maintains the appropriate level of Internal First Loss Capital relative to its External and Senior capital exposures, ensuring sufficient “skin in the game” with respect to its investments.
                    - **Definition of Ratios** (*Core*) - The documents herein define the risk-capital sourcing ratios.
                        - **External Per Internal** **Ratio** *(Core)* - The External Per Internal (EPI) ratio determines the maximum amount of External First Loss Capital (SEFLC and TEFLC combined) that a Star Agent can source directly based on its held Internal First Loss Capital (IFLC). This acts as the primary constraint ensuring a baseline level of the Star's own capital backs its external FLC sourcing. The required External Per Internal ratio is specified in A.3.3 - External Per Internal (EPI) Ratio Value.
                        - **Senior Per First Loss Ratio** *(Core)* - The Senior Per First Loss (SPFL) ratio defines the effectiveness of each type of First Loss Capital (IFLC, SEFLC, TEFLC) in supporting Senior Risk Capital (SRC). Specifically, it quantifies how many units of SRC (whether Originated or Rented) can be activated or "enabled" per unit of a given FLC type.
                            
                            
                            A Star Agent's overall “SPFL capacity” is calculated by summing the potential enablement contributed by each type of FLC it holds (IFLC, SEFLC or TEFLC), where each contribution equals the amount of that FLC type multiplied by its specific SPFL ratio.
                            
                            Since SRC only absorbs losses after FLC is depleted,  the SPFL enablement mechanism validates that sufficient FLC backing exists, allowing enabled SRC to be counted as part of the Star Agent's total eligible Risk Capital. Therefore, a Star Agent might hold a certain amount of OSRC or rented SRC, but only the portion that is successfully "enabled" by its FLC according to the SPFL ratios is accounted by the system as eligible capital contributing towards the Star's satisfaction of its Required Risk Capital. The required ratio of Senior Per First Loss is specified in A.3.3 - Senior Per First Loss (SPFL) Ratio Values. 
                            
                            - **Alternative Use Of SPFL Capacity To Source External First Loss Capital** (Core) - A Star Agent may optionally choose to deploy all or some portion of its calculated SPFL capacity not to enable Senior Risk Capital, but instead to source additional External First Loss Capital. This provides a secondary mechanism to increase the total FLC potentially beyond the amount initially constrained by the EPI ratio applied solely to IFLC.
                                
                                
                                Crucially, the EFLC tranche acquired through utilizing SPFL capacity is permanently accounted for as having an SPFL ratio of zero (0). It contributes to the FLC buffer but provides no capacity itself for enabling SRC in any subsequent calculations. This contrasts with EFLC sourced normally via the EPI ratio, which retains its standard SPFL capacity. 
                                
                    - **Interaction Between Ratios And SPFL Capacity** (*Core*): The sourcing of External First Loss Capital and enablement of Senior Risk Capital involves a sequential application of the risk-capital sourcing ratios in conjunction with the Star Agent’s deployment of its SPFL capacity.
                        
                        
                        First, the EPI ratio sets the initial limit for sourcing standard, SPFL-bearing EFLC based solely on the Star Agent’s IFLC. 
                        
                        Second, the total SPFL capacity is calculated from the Star Agent’s resulting FLC pool (IFLC + any EPI-sourced EFLC). 
                        
                        Finally, this total calculated SPFL capacity can be allocated by the Star Agent to enable SRC (standard use), and/or to source additional, non-SPFL-bearing EFLC using the mechanism described under A.3.3 - Alternative Use Of SPFL Capacity To Source External First Loss Capital. Any specific portion of the total SPFL capacity allocated towards sourcing EFLC cannot simultaneously be allocated towards enabling SRC. 
                        
        - **Implementation** (*Section)* - This Section defines the current implementation of the Risk Capital framework.
            - **Required Risk Capital Calculation Implementation** *(Core)* - The documents herein define the implementation of the risk framework for calculating required Risk Capital.
                - **Financial Risk Implementation** *(Core)* - The documents herein define the implementation of the financial risk framework.
                    - **Asset Class Specific Implementation** *(Core)* - The documents herein define implementations of the financial risk framework for specific asset classes. In the near term, the only implementation that is defined is for lending markets. In the future additional implementations will be added herein.
                        - **Lending Markets** *(Core)* - The documents herein define the implementation of the financial risk framework for lending markets.
                            - **Calculation** *(Core)* - The documents herein define the calculation of the Risk Capital required with respect to a Star Agent’s investments. Unless otherwise specified, code is in Python. Alternative implementations may be used but these implementations must produce the same outputs as the reference implementation included herein.
                                - **Variables** *(Core)* - The documents herein define the variables that are used in the calculation of required Risk Capital.
                                    - **Methodology** *(Core)* - All floating point variables are specified to four decimal places of precision. For example, 100 million would be represented as `100000000.0000` and 75% would be represented as `0.7500`.
                                    - **Input Variables** *(Core)* - Input variables are variables that are input into the model for calculating required Risk Capital, either based on on-chain or off-chain data or reflecting assumptions or parameters set by Governance.
                                        - **Underlying Market** *(Core)* - The `underlying` variable is a string identifying the asset borrowed in a particular lending market. For example, if Dai is borrowed in a lending market, then the value of the `underlying` for that market would be `DAI`.
                                        - **Underlying Market** *(Core)* - The `underlying` variable is a string identifying the asset borrowed in a particular lending market. For example, if Dai is borrowed in a lending market, then the value of the `underlying` for that market would be `DAI`.
                                        - **Collateral Name** *(Core)* - The `collateral_name` variable is a string identifying a particular collateral asset. For example, if WETH is used as collateral, then the value of the `collateral_name` for that collateral would be `WETH`.
                                        - **Debt Backed** *(Core)* - The `debt_backed` variable is a floating point number indicating the amount of the underlying asset borrowed in a market backed by a particular collateral. It is calculated by multiplying (1) the dollar amount of the underlying asset borrowed by (2) the ratio of the dollar value of the collateral to the dollar value of all collaterals across all markets. For example, if there are $30 million of Dai borrowings and $70 million of USDC borrowings that are collectively backed by $240 million of WETH and $60 million of WBTC, then `debt_backed` for the Dai market and the WETH collateral would be $24 million.
                                        - **Collateral** *(Core)* - The `collateral` variable is a floating point number indicating the amount of a particular collateral for a market. It is calculated by multiplying (1) the dollar amount of the collateral by (2) the ratio of dollar borrowings in the market to the dollar value of borrowings across all markets. For example, if there are $30 million of Dai borrowings and $70 million of USDC borrowings that are collectively backed by $240 million of WETH and $60 million of WBTC, then `collateral` for the Dai market and the WETH collateral would be $72 million.
                                        - **Liquidation Threshold** *(Core)* - The `liquidation_threshold` is a floating point number indicating the loan to value point at which liquidation is triggered in a lending market.
                                        - **Liquidation Penalty** *(Core)* - The `liquidation_penalty` is a floating point number representing the percentage of the amount liquidated that is paid to the actor that liquidates an unhealthy loan in a lending market.
                                        - **Liquidation Efficiency Factor** *(Core)* - The `liquidation_efficiency_factor` is an estimate of what percent of the fair market value of the collateral can be recovered in liquidation. The `liquidation_efficiency_factor` is currently set to 75% for all collaterals.
                                        - **Volatility** *(Core)* - The `volatility` for a collateral is a floating point number defined as the standard deviation of daily price changes for the collateral over the last 30 days.
                                        - **Time Horizon** (Core) - The `T` variable is a floating point number indicating the time horizon, in years, over which the Probability Of Default is being estimated. `T` is currently set to one (1) year.
                                    - **Derived Variables** *(Core)* - Derived variables are variables that are calculated based on other variables.
                                        - **Liquidation Ratio** *(Core)* - The `liquidation_ratio` is the value of the collateral for a loan as a percentage of the amount borrowed below which liquidation will be triggered. It is the inverse of the `liquidation_threshold`.
                                        - **Rehypothecation Factor** *(Core)* - The `rehypothecation_factor` is ratio of total borrowing and total collateral for an asset. Total borrowing for an asset is defined as the sum of `debt_backed` where that asset is the `underlying` for all values of `collateral_name`. The total collateral is defined as the sum of the `collateral` variable where the `collateral_name` corresponds to the asset across all values of `underlying`.
                                        - **Relative Debt Backed** *(Core)* - The `relative_debt_backed` for an `underlying` and a `collateral_name` is the `debt_backed` for that `underlying` and `collateral_name` divided by the sum of the `debt_backed` for all `collateral_name` values for the `underlying`. For example, if there are $100 million of Dai borrowings, of which $80 million are backed by WETH and $20 million are backed by WBTC, the `relative_debt_backed` for the Dai market and the WETH collateral would be 80%.
                                        - **Relative Collateral** *(Core)* - The `relative_collateral` is the `collateral` for a particular `underlying` and `collateral_name` divided by the sum of the values for all `collateral` for that `underlying`. For example, if $100 million of Dai borrowings are backed by $300 million of WETH and $100 million of WBTC, the value of the `relative_collateral` for WETH would be 75%.
                                - **Calculating Effective Exposures** *(Core)* - The first stage of the calculation process is calculating effective exposures for each asset, including both first order exposures and second order exposures from rehypothecation.
                                    - **Initial Data** *(Core)* - The process begins with a single table where each row corresponds to a single combination of asset being borrowed ( `underlying` ) and backing collateral (`collateral_name` ). Each row has the following fields:
                                        - `underlying`
                                        - `collateral_name`
                                        - `debt_backed`
                                        - `rehypothecation_factor`
                                        - `collateral`
                                        - `relative_debt_backed`
                                        - `relative_collateral`
                                    - **First Level Computation** *(Core)* - The documents herein define the First Level Computation of exposures, which breaks down exposures into non-rehypothecated and rehypothecated exposures.
                                        - **Methodology** *(Core)* - The calculations specified in the documents below should be performed for each combination of asset being borrowed (`underlying`) and backing collateral (`collateral_name`). The current value of the `collateral_name` for each calculation is referred to as `collateral_1`.
                                        - **Calculation Of Level 1 Rehypothecation Factor** *(Core)* - The Level 1 Rehypothecation Factor (`reh_factor_1`) is set equal to the `rehypothecation_factor`.
                                        - **Calculation Of Level 1 Collateralization** *(Core)* - The Level 1 Collateralization (`coll_1`) is set equal to the `collateralization`.
                                        - **Calculation Of Base Exposure** *(Core)* - The Level 1 Base Exposure (`base_exposure_1`) is calculated as the `debt_backed` times one minus `reh_factor_1`. This represents the portion of the exposure that is not rehypothecated.
                                        - **Calculation Of Level 1 Rehypothecated Exposure** (*Core)* - The Level 1 Rehypothecated Exposure (`reh_exposure_1`) is calculated as the `debt_backed` times `reh_factor_1`. This represents the portion of the exposure that is rehypothecated.
                                        - **Calculation Of Effective Exposure** *(Core)* - The Effective Exposure for the collateral (`effective_exposures[collateral_1]`) is set equal to `base_exposure_1`. This will later be increased as part of the Second Level Computation.
                                        - **Calculation Of Debt Backed Exposure** *(Core)* - The Debt Backed Exposure for the collateral (`debt_backed_exposures[collateral_1]`) is set equal to `debt_backed`. This will later be increased as part of the Second Level Computation.
                                        - **Calculation Of Collateralization** *(Core)* - The Collateralization for the collateral (`collateralization[collateral_1]`) is set equal to `collateral`. This will later be increased as part of the Second Level Computation.
                                    - **Second Level Computation** *(Core)* - The documents herein define the Second Level Computation of exposures, which calculates additional exposures due to rehypothecation.
                                        - **Methodology** *(Core)* - The calculations specified in the documents below should be performed for each asset being borrowed `underlying` and backing collateral `collateral_name`. The current value of the `collateral_name` for each calculation is referred to as `collateral_1`. For each combination of `underlying` and `collateral_name`, the calculation should be performed for each row where the `underlying` is equal to `collateral_1`. The `collateral_name` in such row is referred to as `collateral_2`. This considers the hypothecation exposures where an asset that is collateral is also being borrowed.
                                        - **Calculation Of Level 2 Relative Backing** *(Core)* - The Level 2 Relative Backing (`relvative_backing_2`) is set to the `relative_backing` for `underlying` and `collateral_2`.
                                        - **Calculation Of Level 2 Collateralization Factor** *(Core)* - The Level 2 Collateralization Factor (`coll_factor_2`) is set to the `relative_collateral` for `underlying` and `collateral_2`.
                                        - **Calculation Of Level 2 Effective Exposure** *(Core)* - The Level 2 Effective Exposure (`effective_exposure_2`) is calculated as the product of the Level 1 Rehypothecated Exposure (`reh_exposure_1`) and the Level 2 Relative Backing, (`relative_backing_2`), scaled up by one plus the Level 1 Rehypothecation Factor (`reh_factor_1`).
                                        - **Calculation Of Level 2 Debt Backed** *(Core)* - The Level 2 Debt Backed (`debt_backed_2`) is calculated as the product of the Level 1 Rehypothecated Exposure (`reh_exposure_1`) and the Level 2 Relative Backing (`relative_backing_2`).
                                        - **Calculation Of Level 2 Collateralization** *(Core)* - The Level 2 Collateralization (`coll_backed_2`) is calculated as the product of the Level 1 Collateral, `coll_1` and the Level 2 Collateralization Factor, `coll_factor_2`.
                                        - **Update Of Effective Exposure** *(Core)* - The Effective Exposure for `collateral_2` (`effective_exposures[collateral_2]`) is incremented by `effective_exposure_2`.
                                        - **Update Of Debt Backed Exposure** *(Core)* - The Debt Backed Exposure for `collateral_2` (`debt_backed_exposures[collateral_2]`) is incremented by `debt_backed_2`.
                                        - **Update Of Collateralization** *(Core)* - The Collateralization for the `collateral_2` (`collateralization[collateral_2]`) is incremented by `coll_backed_2`.
                                    - **Aggregation** *(Core)* - The documents herein define the process for aggregating the results from the First Level Computation and Second Level Computation and structuring them for use in later calculations.
                                        - **Collect Outputs From First Level Computation And Second Level Computation** *(Core)* - The final values of `effective_exposures`, `debt_backed_exposures`, and `collateralization` are collected into a new table with one row for each combination of `underlying` and `collateral_name`.  The fields corresponding to `effective_exposures`, `debt_backed_exposures`, and `collateralization` are renamed `effective_exposure`, `debt_backed`, and `effective_coll`, respectively. `underlying` and `collateral_name` are renamed `underlying_symbol` and `final_collateral`, respectively.
                                        - **Attach Additional Information** *(Core)* - The table created above is enriched with the `liquidation_ratio`, `liquidation_penalty`, `rehypothecation_factor`, and `volatility` variables.
                                        - **Recalculate Relative Backing** *(Core)* - A new renormalized `relative_backing` field is calculated by dividing the `effective_exposure` value for each row by the total `effective_exposure` value for all rows with the same `underlying`.
                                    - **Final Data** *(Core)* - As a result of the calculations the final output of this step is a table with one row for each combination of asset being borrowed (`underlying_symbol`) and backing collateral (`final_collateral`). Each row has the following fields:
                                        - `underlying_symbol`
                                        - `final_collateral`
                                        - `effective_exposure`
                                        - `debt_backed`
                                        - `effective_coll`
                                        - `liquidation_ratio`
                                        - `liquidation_penalty`
                                        - `rehypothecation_factor`
                                        - `relative_backing`
                                        - `volatility`
                                    - **Python Implementation** *(Core)* - An implementation of the calculation of effective exposures in Python is shown below:
                                        
                                        ```python
                                        for underlying in all_underlyings:
                                            level1 = df[df['underlying_symbol'] == underlying].copy()
                                        
                                            # Dictionaries to accumulate exposures by collateral.
                                            effective_exposures = {}
                                            debt_backed_exposures = {}
                                            collateralization = {}
                                            
                                           for _, row1 in level1.iterrows():
                                                collateral_1  = row1['collateral_name']
                                                debt_1        = row1['debt_backed']
                                                reh_factor_1  = row1['rehypothecation_factor']
                                                coll_1        = row1['collateral']
                                        
                                                # (A) Non-rehypothecated
                                                base_exposure_1 = debt_1 * (1.0 - reh_factor_1)
                                                effective_exposures[collateral_1] = effective_exposures.get(collateral_1, 0.0) + base_exposure_1
                                                debt_backed_exposures[collateral_1] = debt_backed_exposures.get(collateral_1, 0.0) + debt_1
                                                collateralization[collateral_1] = collateralization.get(collateral_1, 0.0) + coll_1
                                        
                                                # (B) Rehypothecated
                                                reh_exposure_1 = debt_1 * reh_factor_1
                                                # fraction of the "coll_1" that is also being re-lent
                                                collateralization_1 = coll_1 * reh_factor_1
                                                
                                                level2 = df[df['underlying_symbol'] == collateral_1]
                                                for _, row2 in level2.iterrows():
                                                    collateral_2 = row2['collateral_name']
                                                    relative_backing2 = row2['relative_debt_backed']
                                                    coll_factor2 = row2['relative_collateral']
                                        
                                                    # Depth=2 effective exposure
                                                    effective_exposure_2 = reh_exposure_1 * relative_backing2 * (1.0 + reh_factor_1)
                                                    effective_exposures[collateral_2] = (
                                                        effective_exposures.get(collateral_2, 0.0) + effective_exposure_2
                                                    )
                                        
                                                    # Depth=2 debt backed
                                                    debt_backed_2 = reh_exposure_1 * relative_backing2
                                                    debt_backed_exposures[collateral_2] = (
                                                        debt_backed_exposures.get(collateral_2, 0.0) + debt_backed_2
                                                    )
                                        
                                                    # Depth=2 collateralization
                                                    coll_backed_1 = collateralization_1 * coll_factor2
                                                    collateralization[collateral_2] = (
                                                        collateralization.get(collateral_2, 0.0) + coll_backed_1
                                                    )
                                                    
                                           # Combine keys from our three dictionaries:
                                            all_collaterals = set(effective_exposures.keys()) | set(debt_backed_exposures.keys())
                                        
                                            for coll_name in all_collaterals:
                                                res = {
                                                    'underlying_symbol': underlying,
                                                    'final_collateral': coll_name,
                                                    'effective_exposure': effective_exposures.get(coll_name, 0.0),
                                                    'debt_backed': debt_backed_exposures.get(coll_name, 0.0),
                                                    'effective_coll': collateralization.get(coll_name, 0.0)
                                                }
                                        
                                                results.append(res)
                                        ```
                                        
                                - **Calculating Other Effective Parameters** *(Core)* - The second stage of the calculation process involves calculating other effective parameters taking into account the impact of rehypothecation.
                                    - **Methodology** *(Core)* - The documents below define the process for calculating other effective parameters for a single row of the output table from the previous step, corresponding to a combination of an underlying market (`underlying_symbol`) and collateral (`final_collateral`).
                                    - **Effective Volatility** *(Core)* - The documents herein define the process for computing the Effective Volatility.
                                        - **Effective Volatility Calculation** *(Core)* - The Effective Volatility (`effective_volatility`) is calculated for each combination of underlying market and backing collateral using the Python function specified below, where `row` is one row from the output table from calculating effective exposures and `df` is the entire table. This combines the volatility for the non-rehypothecated portion of the collateral with the volatility from the rehypothecated portion of the collateral, based on the volatility of the collaterals that back the rehypothecated portion.
                                            
                                            ```python
                                            def calc_effective_volatility(row, df):
                                                """
                                                Compute effective volatility.
                                                """
                                                w_top = row['relative_backing']           # The fraction of the total backing contributed by this collateral at the top level
                                                f_top = row['rehypothecation_factor']     # How much of this collateral is rehypothecated
                                                top_vol = row['volatility']               # The top-level collateral's own volatility
                                                
                                                # Non-rehypothecated portion
                                                non_reh_vol = (1 - f_top) * top_vol
                                                
                                                # 'M_top' is the magnitude of the portion that is rehypothecated
                                                M_top = w_top * f_top
                                            
                                                # Find "child" rows where this row's final_collateral is itself an underlying_symbol
                                                backing = df[df['underlying_symbol'] == row['final_collateral']].copy()
                                                if backing.empty:
                                                    # If there are no deeper levels for this collateral, the effective volatility is just the top-level's volatility
                                                    return top_vol
                                            
                                                # For each child, define an effective weight (w_eff) = (M_top * [child's relative_backing * child's rehypothecation_factor])
                                                backing['w_eff'] = M_top * (backing['relative_backing'] * backing['rehypothecation_factor'])
                                                
                                                # We model the 'combined' volatility by summing variances from each child, i.e. w_eff^2 * volatility^2
                                                backing['var_contrib'] = (backing['w_eff'] ** 2) * (backing['volatility'] ** 2)
                                                sigma_backing = np.sqrt(backing['var_contrib'].sum())
                                                
                                                # Final effective volatility = sum of top's non_rehypothecated portion + child-level sqrt of variance
                                                return sigma_backing + non_reh_vol
                                            ```
                                            
                                    - **Effective Liquidation Ratio** *(Core)* - The documents herein define the process for computing the Effective Liquidation Ratio.
                                        - **Effective Liquidation Ratio Calculation** *(Core)* - The Effective Liquidation Ratio (`effective_liquidation_ratio`) is calculated for each combination of underlying market and backing collateral using the Python function specified below, where `row` is one row from the output table from calculating effective exposures and `df` is the entire table. This combines the liquidation ratio for the non-rehypothecated portion of the collateral with the liquidation ratio for the rehypothecated portion of the collateral, based on the liquidation ratio of the collaterals that back the rehypothecated portion.
                                            
                                            ```python
                                            def calc_effective_liquidation_ratio(row, df):
                                                """
                                                Compute the effective liquidation ratio.
                                                """
                                                w_top = row['relative_backing']
                                                f_top = row['rehypothecation_factor']
                                                top_liq = row['liquidation_ratio']
                                                
                                                # Non-rehypothecated portion (straight percentage of top-level ratio)
                                                non_reh_liq = (1 - f_top) * top_liq
                                                M_top = w_top * f_top
                                                
                                                # Locate child rows
                                                backing = df[df['underlying_symbol'] == row['final_collateral']].copy()
                                                if backing.empty:
                                                    return top_liq
                                            
                                                # Weighted portion for child rows
                                                backing['w_eff'] = M_top * (backing['relative_backing'] * backing['rehypothecation_factor'])
                                                liq_contrib = (backing['w_eff'] * backing['liquidation_ratio']).sum()
                                                
                                                return non_reh_liq + liq_contrib
                                            ```
                                            
                                    - **Effective Liquidation Penalty** *(Core)* - The documents herein define the process for computing the Effective Liquidation Penalty.
                                        - **Effective Liquidation Penalty Calculation** *(Core)* - The Effective Liquidation Penalty (`effective_liquidation_penalty`) is calculated for each combination of underlying market and backing collateral using the Python function specified below, where `row` is one row from the output table from calculating effective exposures and `df` is the entire table. This combines the liquidation penalty for the non-rehypothecated portion of the collateral with the liquidation penalty for the rehypothecated portion of the collateral, based on the liquidation penalty for the collaterals that back the rehypothecated portion.
                                            
                                            ```python
                                            def calc_effective_liquidation_penalty(row, df):
                                                """
                                                Compute the effective liquidation penalty.
                                                """
                                                w_top = row['relative_backing']
                                                f_top = row['rehypothecation_factor']
                                                top_pen = row['liquidation_penalty']
                                                
                                                non_reh_pen = (1 - f_top) * top_pen
                                                M_top = w_top * f_top
                                                
                                                backing = df[df['underlying_symbol'] == row['final_collateral']].copy()
                                                if backing.empty:
                                                    return top_pen
                                            
                                                backing['w_eff'] = M_top * (backing['relative_backing'] * backing['rehypothecation_factor'])
                                                pen_contrib = (backing['w_eff'] * backing['liquidation_penalty']).sum()
                                                
                                                return non_reh_pen + pen_contrib
                                            ```
                                            
                                - **Calculating Risk Capital Required** *(Core)* - The third stage is calculating the actual Risk Capital required based on the previously calculated variables.
                                    - **Probability Of Default** *(Core)* - The documents herein specify the process for calculating the Probability Of Default.
                                        - **Calculation Of Probability Of Default** *(Core)* - The probability of default (`PD` ) is calculated based on the `effective_liquidation_ratio`, the `effective_collateralization_ratio`, the `effective_volatility`, and `T` using the Python code specified below, which assumes that returns for the collateral are normally distributed.
                                            
                                            ```python
                                            PD = norm.cdf(ln(effective_liquidation_ratio / effective_collateralization_ratio)
                                                            / (effective_volatility * sqrt(T)))
                                            ```
                                            
                                            Here `norm.cdf` is the cumulative distribution function of the standard normal distribution.
                                            
                                    - **Loss Given Default** *(Core)* - The documents herein specify the process for calculating the Loss Given Default.
                                        - **Calculation Of Recovery Rate** *(Core)* - For each underlying market and backing collateral, a Recovery Rate is first calculated. This is equal to the product of (1) the Debt Backed (`debt_backed`), (2) one minus the Effective Liquidation Penalty (`effective_liquidation_penaly`), and (3) the Liquidation Efficiency Factor (`liquidation_efficiency_factor`), divided by the Effective Exposure `effective_exposure`.
                                        - **Calculation Of Loss Given Default** *(Core)* - For each underlying market and backing collateral, the Loss Given Default (`LGD`) is then calculated as one minus the Recovery Rate, with the stipulation that the Loss Given Default cannot be less than zero.
                                        - **Python Implementation** *(Core)* - An implementation of the calculation of the Loss Given Default in Python is shown below:
                                            
                                            ```python
                                            LGD = max(
                                                1 - (
                                                    debt_backed
                                                    * effective_liquidation_ratio
                                                    * (1 - effective_liquidation_penalty)
                                                    * liquidation_efficiency_factor
                                                    / effective_exposure
                                                ),
                                                0
                                            )
                                            ```
                                            
                                    - **Asset Correlation Coefficient** *(Core)* - The documents herein specify the process for calculating the Asset Correlation Coefficient.
                                        - **Asset Correlation Coefficient Calculation** *(Core)* - The Asset Correlation Coefficient (`ACC`) for each underlying market and collateral is calculated using the formula specified in the Python code below as a function of the Probability Of Default (`PD`).
                                            
                                            ```python
                                            X   = (1 - exp(-50 * PD)) / (1 - exp(-50))
                                            ACC = 0.12 * X + 0.24 * (1 - X)
                                            ```
                                            
                                    - **Credit Risk** *(Core)* - The documents herein specify the process for calculating Credit Risk.
                                        - **Calculation Of Shifted Probability Of Default** *(Core)* - The Shifted Probability Of Default (`shifted_PD`) for an underlying market and collateral is calculated as a function of the Probability Of Default (`PD`) and the Asset Correlation Coefficient (`ACC`) using the Python code below. This estimates the probability of default under a “stressed” scenario.
                                            
                                            ```python
                                            shifted_PD = norm.cdf(
                                                ( norm.ppf(PD) + sqrt(ACC) * norm.ppf(0.999) )
                                                / sqrt(1 - ACC)
                                            )
                                            ```
                                            
                                        - **Calculation Of Credit Risk** *(Core)* - The Credit Risk (`credit_risk`) for an underlying market and collateral is calculated as the difference between the Shifted Probability Of Default (`shifted_PD`) and the Probability Of Default (`PD`), multiplied by the Loss Given Default (`LGD`). This is shown in the Python code below.
                                            
                                            ```python
                                            
                                            credit_risk = (shifted_PD - PD) * LGD
                                            ```
                                            
                                    - **Risk Weighted Assets** *(Core)* - The documents herein specify the process for calculating Risk Weighted Assets.
                                        - **Calculation Of Weighted Credit Risk** *(Core)* - The Weighted Credit Risk (`weighted_credit_risk(u)`) for each underlying market (`u`) is calculated as the sum of the product of the Relative Backing (`relative_backing(i)`) and the Credit Risk (`credit_risk(i)`) for each collateral (`i`) backing that market.
                                            
                                            ```markdown
                                            weighted_credit_risk(u) = sum(relative_backing(i) * credit_risk(i)) for all collaterals i backing u
                                            ```
                                            
                                        - **Total Debt Backed** *(Core)* - The Total Debt Backed (`total_debt_backed(u)`) for each underlying market (`u`) is calculated as the sum of the Debt Backed (`debt_backed(i)`) for each collateral (`i`) backing that market.
                                            
                                            ```markdown
                                            total_debt_backed(u) = sum(debt_backed(i)) for all collaterals i backing u
                                            ```
                                            
                                        - **Calculation Of Risk Weighted Assets** *(Core)* - The Risk Weighted Assets (`RWA(u)`) for each market (`u`) is calculated as the product of the Total Debt Backed (`total_debt_backed(u)`) and the Weighted Credit Risk (`weighted_credit_risk(u)`).
                                            
                                            ```markdown
                                            RWA(u) = total_debt_backed(u) * weighted_credit_risk(u)
                                            ```
                                            
                                    - **Risk Capital Required** *(Core)* - Finally, the Risk Capital (`FLC(u)`) required for each market `u` is the Risk Weighted Assets (`RWA(u)`) multiplied by three times 2.75%, reflecting that 100% weight is being placed on Credit Risk and 0% weight on Market Risk and Liquidity Risk.
                                        
                                        ```markdown
                                        FLC(u) = RWA(u) * (0.0275 * 3)
                                        ```
                                        
                                        The total Risk Capital required across all markets is simply the sum of the Risk Capital required for each market.

                - **External Senior Risk Capital And srUSDS System** *(Core)* - The documents herein define rules and infrastructure pertaining to the sourcing of External Senior Risk Capital (ESRC). ESRC is capital provided by external investors to be invested as Senior Risk Capital of Star Agents.
                    - **srUSDS Contract** *(Core)* - srUSDS is the tokenized form of External Senior Risk Capital. Users can deposit USDS into the srUSDS contract to provide External Senior Risk Capital to the SRC system, making it available for origination by Star Agents. See A.3.3 - Senior Risk Capital (SRC) Origination Process. srUSDS holders can potentially earn higher yields in exchange for the risk that the External Senior Risk Capital will suffer losses.
                    - **Deposit And Redemption Queues** (*Core)* - The srUSDS contract utilizes distinct queues for managing conversions. Users can add USDS to the deposit queue for conversion into srUSDS; or add srUSDS to the redemption queue for conversion back into USDS at any point during the month.
                        
                        
                        Assets placed in either queue can be withdrawn by the user at any time before the Monthly Settlement Cycle begins.
                        
                        At the Monthly Settlement Cycle, all assets remaining in the queues are processed: queued USDS is converted into srUSDS, and queued srUSDS is converted into USDS based on the prevailing Conversion Rate. See A.3.3 - Conversion Rate.
                        
                    - **Conversion Rate** *(Core)* - The conversion rate represents the amount of USDS redeemable per unit of srUSDS. This rate increases or decreases over time, reflecting the net return earned by the ESRC pool. The rate is updated at the conclusion of each Monthly Settlement Cycle based on the performance of the ESRC pool during the preceding month, according to the following standard compounding formula:
                        
                        `New srUSDS Conversion Rate = Previous Conversion Rate * (1 + Monthly srUSDS Yield)`
                        
                        The parameters of this formula are defined in the documents herein.
                        
                        - **Previous Conversion Rate** *(Core)*  - `Previous Conversion Rate` is the conversion rate (USDS per srUSDS) that was in effect at the beginning of the concluded monthly cycle. The initial conversion rate between srUSDS and USDS is 1:1.
                        - **Monthly srUSDS Yield** (*Core*) - `Monthly srUSDS Yield` represents the net percentage return earned solely by the ESRC pool during the concluded monthly cycle, distributable to srUSDS holders via the conversion rate adjustment. It directly reflects the risk-adjusted performance of the capital deployed and is calculated as:
                            
                            `[(Total Interest Paid by Stars on ESRC Portion - Losses Allocated to ESRC) / ESRC Principal at start of month - Sky Spread] * (1 - ESRC Earnings Fee)`
                            
                            The Sky Spread and ESRC Earnings Fee are defined in A.3.3 - Sky Spread and A.3.3 - ESRC Earnings Fee, respectively.
                            
                        - **Sources Of Revenue To Sky** *(Core)* - Sky earns revenue from facilitating ESRC from two sources. These sources are included in the formula and defined in the subdocuments below.
                            - **Sky Spread** *(Core)* - Sky takes a spread equal to the Sky Spread on all ESRC balances. The Sky Spread is the difference between (1) the rate at which Star Agents borrow from Sky and (2) the Sky Savings Rate plus the Accessibility Reward Fee.
                            - **ESRC Earnings Fee** *(Core*): Sky takes a 5% fee on the net interest earnings generated from the ESRC portion of originated SRC before these earnings are distributed to srUSDS holders via the conversion rate adjustment.
                    - **srUSDS Accessibility Reward** *(Core)* - Star Agents and third-party partners that drive srUSDS usage are entitled to a srUSDS Accessibility Reward similar to the Accessibility Reward paid on USDS balances. The srUSDS Accessibility Reward Fee is the same as the Accessibility Reward Fee on USDS. See [A.2.4 - Accessibility Reward Primitive](https://sky-atlas.powerhouse.io/A.2.3.8.1_Accessibility_Reward_Primitive/1b3f2ff0-8d73-80b4-873c-ec532edc750c|9e1ff936eafd).
                - **Senior Risk Capital (SRC) Origination Process** *(Core)* - This document defines the operational details of the recurring monthly process allowing Star Agents to originate Senior Risk Capital (Originated Senior Risk Capital or OSRC) from the Total Senior Risk Capital (TSRC) pool. The process involves determining the available TSRC supply, a bidding phase, an allocation auction, and settlement aligned with the Monthly Settlement Cycle.
                    - **Process Timing and Schedule** *(Core)* - The SRC origination process operates monthly, synchronized with the Monthly Settlement Cycle. The bidding window occurs during the current month for SRC that is usable in the next month; the bidding window closes before settlement processing begins. Additional operational details, including timelines, will be specified in a future iteration of the Atlas.
                    - **Available Total Senior Risk Capital Pool Determination** *(Core)* - Total Senior Risk Capital (TSRC) is the amount of Senior Risk Capital that is available each Monthly Settlement Cycle for Stars to originate and thus turn into Originated Senior Risk Capital (OSRC) through the monthly origination process. TSRC is the sum of designated Internal Senior Risk Capital (ISRC) from Sky surplus buffers and available External Senior Risk Capital (ESRC) from the srUSDS contract. Prior to each monthly bidding window, the TSRC is calculated and publicly announced. Additional operational details will be defined in a future iteration of the Atlas.
                    - **Bidding Process** *(Core)* - Participating Star Agents submit bids via the Powerhouse interface during the defined bidding window. Each bid must specify: (1) The quantity of SRC the Star seeks to originate, and (2) The maximum price the Star is willing to pay (expressed in basis points above the Base Rate) for using the OSRC during the upcoming monthly period. Additional operational details will be specified in a future iteration of the Atlas.
                    - **Allocation & Clearing Price Determination** *(Core)* - Bids are ranked descending by price. TSRC is allocated sequentially to the highest bids until the pool is exhausted or all bids are filled. The price of the lowest successful bid that receives an allocation (even partial) sets the uniform clearing price for the month. All winning bidders pay this clearing price per unit of OSRC allocated, regardless of their original bid price. Additional operational details will be specified in a future iteration of the Atlas.
                    - **Settlement Of Origination** *(Core)* - During the Monthly Settlement Cycle following allocation, the cost (Allocated OSRC Quantity * Clearing Price) is deducted from each winning Star Agent’s operational account. Concurrently, the allocated OSRC amount is credited to the Star Agent’s Risk Capital account as OSRC for the upcoming monthly period.
                    - **OSRC Duration and Renewal** *(Core)* - OSRC is valid only for the single monthly period following the settlement cycle in which it was originated. Renewal requires successful participation in the next monthly origination process; there is no automatic rollover.
                - **Originated Senior Risk Capital (OSRC) Rental Implementation** *(Core)* - The operational process for inter-Star Agent OSRC risk-capital rentals is defined herein.                         - **Responsibility For Cost Of Real Time Updates** *(Core)* - Star Agents may develop data feeds that update the Atlas with their data at their expense. Alternatively, the Operational Executor Agent that has an Executor Accord with the Star Agent may perform this as part of the services it provides to the Star Agent. In this case, the Operational Executor Agent may charge whatever fees for this service the parties mutually agree on.
                            - **Near Term Real Time Updates By BA Labs** *(Core)* - In the near term, BA Labs will provide real time data regarding major lending markets. BA Labs will be compensated for this work as part of its existing relationship with Sky Core and Spark.
                    - **Non-Real Time Updates** *(Core)* - In the near term, Star Agents may update calculations that are based on market or other external variables less frequently than real time. If they do so, Operational GovOps must apply a buffer to each variable that is not updated in real time to account for the fact that the variable may change in a way that increases the Risk Capital requirements between update frequencies.
                - **Data Maintenance And Integrity** *(Core)* - The documents herein define processes to maintain and ensure the integrity of data that is used as an input to Risk Capital calculations.
                    - **Responsibility For Data Updates** *(Core)* - The Star Agent may assume responsibility for updating the inputs to the Risk Capital calculations itself, either directly or through one of its agents, or may contract with Operational GovOps to perform that function.
                    - **Verification Of Data Updates** *(Core)* - If Operational GovOps does not perform the data updates, it must verify their accuracy.
                    - **Penalties For Late Data Updates** *(Core)* - In the near term, Operational GovOps must reduce exposure to any Asset Allocation Conduit if the Star Agent responsible for that Conduit fails to provide the timely information necessary to calculate the required Risk Capital. In the future, a system of monetary penalties must be developed to compensate Sky for the risk posed by late data submission.
            - **Process For Adjusting Risk Capital** (Core) - The documents herein define how changes to the required amount of Risk Capital should be handled.
                - **Updates Due To Changed Market Parameters** *(Core)* - The documents herein define the process for handling changes to Risk Capital requirements due to changes in market or other external variables.
                    - **Immediate Update To Risk Capital Requirements For Changes In Market Or External Variables** *(Core)* - To the extent that a market or external variable changes in a way that increases the required Risk Capital, the Star Agent is immediately responsible for supplying the additional Risk Capital and is subject to penalties for any period in which this Risk Capital is not provided. It is the responsibility of the Star Agent to maintain sufficient capital so that they remain well capitalized in the face of changes to market conditions.
                - **Updates Due To Changed Methodology Or Governance Parameters** *(Core)* - The documents herein define the process for adjusting Risk Capital requirements whenever Sky Governance modifies the underlying methodology/parameters governing FLC calculations, thereby altering the FLC each Star Agent must hold.
                    - **Phased Update To Risk Capital Requirements For Changes In Methodology Or Governance Parameters** *(Core)* - To the extent that a change to the implementation of the Risk Capital framework or a parameter set by Sky Governance changes the Risk Capital requirements, the additional Risk Capital required will phase in over time. The Star Agent must provide 50% of the additional Risk Capital within seven (7) calendar days of the change, and the remaining 50% on a pro rata basis over the following twenty one (21) calendar days.
                    - **No Penalties For Changes In Methodology Or Governance Parameters In Near Term** *(Core)* - In the near term as the Risk Capital implementation continues to be developed, Star Agents will not be subject to penalties for failing to supply the incremental capital required by changes to the Risk Capital implementation. However, Operational GovOps must reduce the exposure to Allocation Conduits managed by the Star Agent to bring it back into compliance with capital requirements if the Star Agent does not work in good faith to provide the additional capital on the timeline specified above.
            - **Monitoring And Penalty Mechanisms** *(Core)* - The documents herein define monitoring and penalty mechanisms.
                - **Monitoring As Part Of Settlement Cycle** *(Core)* - Core GovOps reviews the calculation of Risk Capital by each Star Agent as part of the Settlement Cycle. In the event that it detects that Risk Capital requirements were violated, it applies the penalty mechanisms specified in A.3.3 - Penalty Mechanisms.
                - **Penalty Mechanisms** *(Core)* - The documents herein specify the penalty mechanisms that exist to incentivize Star Agents and Operational Agents to ensure that sufficient capital is always held against investments by Star Agents and to protect Sky from risks due to insufficient capital.
                    - **Penalties Mechanisms For Star Agents** *(Core)* - The documents herein specify the penalty mechanisms for Star Agents for breaches of the Risk Capital requirements.
                        - **Financial Penalties For Breach Of Capital Requirements** *(Core)* - Core GovOps may impose a financial penalty on the Star Agent for failing to satisfy the Risk Capital requirements. In the near term, Core GovOps may determine a reasonable penalty taking into account (1) the size of the capital shortfall, (2) the length of its occurrence, (3) the ability of the Star Agent to pay, and (4) any other circumstances it deems relevant. Future iterations of the Risk Capital framework will specify a more detailed methodology for calculating penalties.
                        - **Additional Token Issuance For Breach Of Capital Requirements** *(Core)* - In the event that financial penalties are not sufficient to address the shortfall because payment of the penalties would exacerbate an existing capital shortfall, Core GovOps may require the Star Agent to issue additional tokens and sell them to the extent it deems necessary. Operational GovOps will assist Core GovOps in executing any such transaction. Future iterations of the Risk Capital framework will specify a more detailed methodology for required token issuance.
                        - **Restrictions On Investments For Breach Of Capital Requirements** *(Core)* - Core GovOps may impose whatever restrictions it deems necessary on the usage of the Allocation System Primitive by the Star Agent, including limiting the Star Agent’s exposure to certain Allocation Conduits or prohibiting the Star Agent from using certain Allocation Conduits. These restrictions may extend for a period of time that Core GovOps deems fit.
                        - **Conservatorship For Breach Of Capital Requirements** *(Core)* - In the event that less extreme measures are not adequate to address the capital shortfall, Core GovOps may seek to put the Star Agent into conservatorship, in which case Sky Core Facilitators take direct control of the Star Agent to maximize value for Sky and other Star Agent stakeholders. Seeking to put a Star Agent into conservatorship requires immediate escalation to Sky Core Governance and requires an expedited Executive Vote as specified in [A.1.8 - Emergency Voting System - Emergency Response Process - Known and Uncontentious Remedies](https://sky-atlas.powerhouse.io/A.1.8.1.5.4.1_Known_And_Uncontentious_Remedies/28e7fc7a-9bed-4a3c-ad82-e633f34e8b22|0db3140b840b7e9adad0).
                    - **Penalty Mechanisms For Operational Executor Agents** *(Core)* - The documents herein specify the penalty mechanisms for Operational Executor Agents for breaches of Risk Capital requirements.
                        - **Financial Penalties For Operational Executor Agents** *(Core)* - In the near term there are no penalties for Operational Executor Agents for failure of the Star Agents they supervise to maintain appropriate levels of capital. In future iterations of the Risk Framework, Operational Executor Agents will be required to put up collateral and act as “insurers” of all activities by the Star Agents they supervise with regard to the Sky Primitives. At that point Core GovOps will be able to seize this collateral in the event of failure to obtain compensation for breaches of the Risk Capital requirement by the Star Agent.
                        - **Termination Of Executor Accord** *(Core)* - In the event that Core GovOps determines that the Operational Executor Agent is not appropriately supervising the activities of the Star Agent, Core GovOps may terminate the respective Executor Accord. This requires an immediate escalation to Sky Governance and requires an expedited Executive Vote as specified in [A.1.8 - Emergency Voting System - Emergency Response Process - Known and Uncontentious Remedies](https://sky-atlas.powerhouse.io/A.1.8.1.5.4.1_Known_And_Uncontentious_Remedies/28e7fc7a-9bed-4a3c-ad82-e633f34e8b22|0db3140b840b7e9adad0). Core GovOps must arrange for another Operational Executor Agent to stand in until the Star Agent can Invoke the Executor Accord Primitive to enter into a new Executor Accord. The Star Agent must suspend all operations until a new Executor Accord is voted on and formally integrated into the Agent’s Artifact.
                    - **Escalation To Sky Governance** *(Core)* - The documents herein specify measures for escalating situations directly to Sky Governance in the event that they cannot be resolved by Core GovOps.
                        - **Triggers For Escalation** *(Core)* - In the event of a disagreement regarding the penalties for a violation of Risk Capital requirements, either party may escalate the matter to Sky Governance. In addition, the extraordinary remedies of Star Agent Conservatorship and Executor Accord Termination must always be escalated to a Sky Governance Executive Vote.
                        - **Sky Governance Process** *(Core)* - Once a matter has been escalated to Sky Governance, the Sky Core Facilitators may request any information they deem necessary from Core GovOps, Operational GovOps, and the Star Agent. Sky Governance then acts through a Governance Poll to determine its resolution of the matter.
